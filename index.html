Incidents by political era

UPA era (2004–May 2014): 24. NDA era (May 2014–now): 86. Longer NDA window + digitisation are major confounders — not a like-for-like comparison.

Exam-cancellation rate by era

Share of incidents where the exam was cancelled/retest ordered: 17% (UPA) vs 62% (NDA) — response has hardened over time, partly due to the 2024 Public Examinations Act.

Incident rate per year, normalized by era length

UPA era spans ~10.1 years (2004–May 2014) → 24 incidents ≈ 2.4/year. NDA era spans ~12.2 years (May 2014–2026) → 86 incidents ≈ 7.2/year — roughly a 3x rise in the annual rate, not just a raw count. Caveat: part of this rise reflects better reporting/digitisation of older incidents rather than a purely real increase in leaks — the dataset cannot fully separate the two effects. Still, the sharp 2021–2024 clustering (52 incidents in 4 years alone) suggests the rise is not purely a reporting artifact.

Source & provenance

Data: "India Paper Leaks from 2004 to 2026" — Kaggle dataset by Sujay Nadkarni (paper_leaks.csv, 110 rows / 18 columns: incident_id, date, era, exam_name, conducting_body, body_type, area, leak_status, action_taken, note, arrests, convictions, aspirants_affected, linked_deaths, deaths_note, source_name, source_url, confidence).

Each row in the underlying CSV is individually sourced and confidence-rated (High/Medium/Low) with a direct source_url (CBI/court filings, The Tribune, PTI, India TV, The Week, icbse.com, The420.in, etc.) — the dataset compiler cross-checked incidents against press and legal records rather than aggregating unverified claims. This dashboard aggregates that dataset; it does not add outside claims. Figures here can be independently re-derived from the same CSV.
Built from paper_leaks.csv (110 rows, 18 columns), sourced from the Kaggle dataset linked above. Death-toll figures in the source data are explicitly flagged as contested press tallies, not official causal counts — omitted here for that reason.
